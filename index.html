Interpretable machine learning

Understanding what agents learn.

I study how learned representations shape an agent’s decisions—and how to tell whether an agent has learned the requirements its behavior depends on.

I am a Ph.D. student at Georgia Tech. My work brings together interpretable machine learning, structured policy representations, and hierarchical reinforcement learning. I am interested in extending these methods to vision-language-action models (VLAs) and embodied systems: understanding what foundation-model policies represent, how they use it, and when their decisions remain reliable.

Research interests

Sequential decision-making

Hierarchical reinforcement learning, skill preconditions, and the distinction between learned behavior and externally enforced constraints.

Vision-language-action models

Interpretability and evaluation of VLAs, with an interest in transferring structured decision-making methods to foundation-model policies for embodied agents.

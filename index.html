Shalini Sushri

PhD student in the School of Interactive Computing and NSF/CRA CSGrad4US Fellow.

I work on interpretable and trustworthy machine learning for sequential decision-making. My research studies when compact, human-readable policy representations preserve the information an agent needs to act, and how structured representations can make learned behavior more verifiable. I am particularly interested in reinforcement learning, hierarchical policies, concept-based representations, and vision-language-action models for embodied agents.

Research

Interpretable & Trustworthy Sequential Decision-Making

Methods for determining whether learned policies encode decision-relevant concepts, constraints, and structure rather than relying on opaque or externally scaffolded behavior.

Structured Policy Representations

Concept bottlenecks, decision trees, skill hierarchies, and other compact representations that expose how a policy organizes and uses information while preserving task performance.

Embodied AI & Vision-Language-Action Models

Interpretability and safety questions for embodied policies, including how learned representations and external constraints interact in long-horizon robot decision-making.
